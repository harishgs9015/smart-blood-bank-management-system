How Blood Donation Works in India

Understanding the national voluntary donation framework, component separation, and ABO/Rh compatibility.

1. The Voluntary Donation Framework

In India, blood transfusion services are governed by the Drugs and Cosmetics Act (1940) and the National Blood Transfusion Council (NBTC). Commercial blood selling is strictly prohibited by the Supreme Court of India since 1998; all blood must be sourced via voluntary non-remunerated donors.

Our digital management system connects voluntary donors directly with licensed blood centers. This eliminates artificial middlemen, safeguards donor health intervals, prevents double-booking at camps, and provides verifiable stock transparency.

Mandatory Testing Protocol (India)

Every donated unit of blood undergoes mandatory testing before release for transfusion:

  • HIV 1 & 2 antibodies and p24 antigen screening
  • Hepatitis B (HBV) surface antigen (HBsAg)
  • Hepatitis C (HCV) antibody screening
  • Malarial Parasites peripheral smear / rapid test
  • Syphilis (VDRL / TPHA) screening

Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC)

Carries oxygen to tissues. Stored at 2°C to 6°C for up to 35-42 days. Critical for anemia, trauma, childbirth hemorrhage, and surgeries.

Platelet Concentrates (RDP/SDP)

Essential for blood clotting. Stored at 20°C to 24°C under continuous agitation for only 5 days. Critical for Dengue patients, oncology, and leukemia.

Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)

Contains vital clotting factors and proteins. Stored frozen at -30°C for up to 1 year. Used in severe burns, liver disease, and coagulopathy.
